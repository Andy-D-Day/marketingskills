## [HOOK] 0:00–0:40

<!-- [CLIP] Strong standalone opener -->

Agency founders come to me all the time telling me the same thing.

"I've built AI into my agency. I've automated half my workflows. My margins are insane. This is going to be worth so much more when I sell."

And every single time, I have to give them the same hard truth.

**Nobody is paying a bigger multiple because you're using AI.**

I've done this long enough to have seen hundreds of agency exits. And right now, in 2025 and into 2026, this AI valuation myth is costing founders millions of dollars in misaligned expectations. So today I'm going to break it all down — what AI actually does to your agency, what it doesn't do, and the one move that genuinely changes your exit number.

Let's get into it.

## [CREDIBILITY + SETUP] 0:40–1:30

I run a mergers and acquisitions advisory, and we work specifically with marketing and digital agencies. I've had thousands of conversations with agency founders — from boutique SEO shops to $50M media groups — and I've watched this industry go through a fundamental shift in the last 18 months.

AI is everywhere now. It's not a differentiator anymore. It's table stakes.

And the founders who understand that early are the ones who actually position their agencies well for exit. The ones who don't — they're the ones sitting across from buyers, confused about why the offer came in half of what they expected.

## [PART 1: AI IS IMPROVING MARGINS — THAT PART IS REAL] 1:30–3:30

<!-- [CLIP] The margin story — standalone insight -->

Let me be clear: AI genuinely is improving agency margins. That is real.

I spoke with Jeff Conlon — runs an agency out of San Francisco. He built custom GPTs trained on his clients' content. One person on his team now does in 10 hours what used to take 40. Think about what that does to your EBITDA. That's not incremental improvement, that's a restructuring of your cost base.

He also built voice agents for a luxury showroom client. That client eliminated an entire team member just for appointment scheduling. A human being. Gone. Replaced by an agent that never calls in sick, never takes a lunch break, works 24/7.

I've talked to media agencies that are automating influencer discovery, CPM calculation, fee negotiation. Entire workflows that used to require teams of coordinators — now running on agents.

And I had one conversation where we talked about agencies churning out 20,000 pieces of content per week with AI-driven systems. 20,000 pieces. The volume play is real.

So yes — if you're using AI inside your agency, your margins are probably going up. Your headcount is probably going down. You're doing more with less.

That's genuinely good for your business.

But here's where the trap lives.

---

## [PART 2: THE TRAP — "I USE AI SO I'M WORTH MORE"] 3:30–5:30

<!-- [CLIP] The core counterintuitive insight — high clip potential -->

Higher margins do not automatically mean a higher valuation.

I know. That sounds wrong. But stay with me.

I was talking to Nick Koumaris late last year. Smart founder, growing agency, had really leaned into AI tools internally. He was building a case for why his multiple should be higher. And I told him straight:

**"Everybody's using AI now. That's not a differentiator. That's the baseline."**

When a buyer sits down to value your agency, they're not asking "do you use AI?" They're asking: what's the risk of buying this? How stable is the revenue? What happens when the founder leaves? Is there a real management team?

Using AI internally answers none of those questions.

In fact — and this is the counterintuitive part — sky-high margins can actually hurt your valuation.

I had this exact conversation with Jeff. His margins were getting really strong, and he thought that was pure upside. I had to explain: if your EBITDA margin is 50, 55, 60 percent, a sophisticated buyer looks at that and thinks — "there's no management team here. This is one person running everything with bots." 

And that's a risk. Not an asset.

I'll say it plainly: a buyer is more likely to pay a 10x multiple for an agency with 20–30% margins that has a real team, scaled processes, real infrastructure — than they are to pay for a one-person operation with 50% margins, even if the numbers look great on paper.

Because they're not just buying your profit. They're buying a business they can operate without you.

AI that replaces your team doesn't give them that. It gives them a fragile system with a single point of failure.

---

## [PART 3: INTERNAL TOOLS DON'T CHANGE YOUR MULTIPLE] 5:30–7:00

<!-- [CLIP] The "front door" rule — clean, punchy standalone -->

I had a conversation with Cliff Simmons earlier in the year. He'd built some impressive internal tooling — AI-powered reporting, automated client dashboards. He genuinely believed it would push his multiple up. 

I had to tell him: only if it's client-facing and revenue-generating.

There's a rule I use: **Does it live at the front door of your business, or is it just inside the walls?**

Edward Fuller — brilliant guy, runs a media agency called Media Bodies — built an AI system that cut his reporting time from 10 hours per report down to 30 seconds. Incredible internal efficiency.

But I told him: that tool needs to generate revenue for it to change your valuation. If you're not running clients through it, if it's not on your pricing sheet, if buyers can't see it generating cash — then to them, it's just a cost-saving measure.

Cost savings show up in your EBITDA. That's it.

That's not nothing — it moves your margin, which moves your EBITDA, which moves the absolute value of a given multiple. But it doesn't move the **multiple itself**.

The only thing that moves the multiple is changing the category of business you're selling.

---

## [PART 4: WHAT ACTUALLY DOES CREATE VALUE — THE PRODUCTIZATION PLAY] 7:00–8:30

<!-- [CLIP] The 13.5x story — the big payoff moment, very high clip potential -->

Here's where it gets interesting. Because AI can absolutely change your exit — if you do one specific thing with it.

You turn it into a product.

Agency multiples typically land at 4 to 6 times EBITDA. That's the range. Good agency, clean books, strong retention — maybe you push to 7x if conditions are right.

SaaS multiples? We're talking 10x revenue. Not EBITDA — revenue.

That's a completely different universe.

I sold an agency — I won't give all the details — but the founders thought they were walking away with £6 to £7 million. They had a strong agency, but they'd also built a platform. A client-facing software tool with its own subscription revenue.

That deal closed at £13.5 million.

The platform changed everything. It wasn't just an agency anymore. Part of the valuation was priced as a SaaS business. The blended multiple reflected two different asset classes.

That's the play.

If you have an AI capability that your clients would pay a monthly fee to access — if you can wrap it in a product interface, give it a pricing page, build recurring subscription revenue — now you're in a different conversation with buyers entirely.

The question to ask yourself: Is there anything I've built internally that my clients would pay for directly?

If the answer is yes — and with AI tooling, more founders are finding the answer is yes — that is where you focus your energy before you go to market.

---

## [PART 5: THE COMMODITIZATION THREAT] 8:30–9:30

<!-- [CLIP] The "kid in a bedroom" line — provocative, high share potential -->

One more thing I want to address because it matters for how you think about your agency's positioning going forward.

AI is not just changing margins. It's threatening entire categories of agency work.

The big holding companies — WPP, IPG, the old guard — they're cutting 10, 20, 30 percent of their workforces because the volume work is gone. A kid in Dubai with a laptop and the right AI stack can match what an entire department used to produce.

Content studios that built their businesses on volume? At the highest risk. The expensive New York studio with the fancy office, churning out e-commerce product photos — they're competing with someone in a bedroom running Midjourney at scale.

The work that AI cannot replace is the work that requires physical presence, real relationships, and genuine human experience.

Immersive experiential. Creator economy at the high end. Brand experiences that physically put people in a room — Netflix pop-up cinemas, Nike branded courts for NBA activations. Brands are paying three to five million dollars for a one-week pop-up. That can't be faked with an AI agent.

If you're in a commoditizable service category, the time to reposition is now. Before the buyer realizes it too.

---

## [CLOSE + CTA] 9:30–10:00

<!-- [CLIP] Clean summary closer -->

So let me land this cleanly.

AI is a margin tool. Full stop. It makes your operations more efficient. It cuts your cost base. It improves your EBITDA in absolute terms.

But it does not move your multiple.

The only way AI changes your valuation is if you productize it — turn it into a client-facing, revenue-generating product that earns subscription income. Then you're not selling an agency anymore. You're selling a hybrid. And hybrids get hybrid multiples.

If you're thinking about an exit in the next two to four years, that's the question you need to be sitting with right now.

What can I build with AI that my clients would pay for directly?

That's it. That's the whole game.
