# Consulting Agency Roll-Up Strategy: How to Build a Mid-Market Consulting Platform Through Acquisition

Consulting is the most people-dependent business in professional services. The product is intellectual capital, and it walks out the door every night. This makes consulting firms both incredibly attractive and uniquely risky roll-up targets.

The opportunity is clear: the consulting market is bifurcated. Massive firms (McKinsey, Bain, Deloitte) dominate the top. Thousands of boutique firms doing $500K-$5M compete at the bottom. The middle — firms doing $10M-$50M with specialized expertise and professional operations — is dramatically underserved. A roll-up fills that gap.

The risk is equally clear: when you acquire a consulting firm, you're buying relationships and expertise attached to specific people. If those people leave, you've bought an empty office.

This guide covers why consulting firms are compelling roll-up targets despite the people risk, how to structure deals that protect against talent departure, where to find targets, how to finance acquisitions, integration approaches that work for knowledge businesses, and how to build a mid-market consulting platform.

## Why Consulting Firms Are Compelling Roll-Up Targets

### The Market Gap

| Segment | Revenue | Characteristics |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Elite (MBB + Big 4) | $1B+ | Global, brand-driven, generalist + practice areas |
| Upper mid-market | $50M-$500M | Regional/national, specialized, PE-backed |
| **The gap** | **$10M-$50M** | **Underserved. Too big for boutique, too small for elite.** |
| Boutique | $500K-$5M | Partner-led, 5-30 people, specialized, fragmented |
| Solo/micro | < $500K | Individual consultants, fractional executives |

The $10M-$50M segment is where roll-ups create value. Clients in this tier want:
- Broader expertise than a boutique can offer
- More attention than a Big 4 will give a mid-market engagement
- Industry specialization with enough bench depth for large projects
- Competitive rates (not Big 4 pricing)

No individual boutique can serve this need. A platform of combined boutiques can.

### PE Loves Consulting Roll-Ups

Private equity firms have a long track record of successful consulting roll-ups:

- **Huron Consulting Group**: Built through acquisition from $0 to $1B+ revenue
- **FTI Consulting**: Systematic acquisitions across multiple consulting disciplines
- **Alvarez & Marsal**: Grew through acquisitions to become a global restructuring leader
- **West Monroe**: PE-backed, grew through acquisition of technology and management consulting firms

The model is proven. PE investors understand consulting economics and are willing to back consolidation plays with significant capital.

### Recurring Revenue Is Growing

Traditionally, consulting was pure project/engagement work. But the market is shifting:

- **Advisory retainers**: Monthly access to senior expertise, $5K-$25K/month
- **Fractional C-suite**: Fractional CMO, CFO, CTO arrangements, typically 6-12 month commitments
- **Managed services**: Outsourced functions (managed IT, managed finance operations)
- **Subscription research**: Industry insights and benchmarking data on annual subscription

Firms with 30%+ recurring revenue command significantly higher multiples than pure project-based consultancies.

## The Roll-Up Thesis: Building a "Mid-Market McKinsey"

### Multiple Arbitrage

| Firm Revenue | Typical EBITDA Multiple | Why |
|-------------|------------------------|-----|
| $500K-$1M | 2-3x | Partner-dependent, small bench, limited brand |
| $1M-$3M | 3-4x | Still partner-led, some junior talent leverage |
| $3M-$5M | 4-5x | Growing team, some institutionalized client relationships |
| $5M-$10M | 5-7x | Professional management, diversified partnerships |
| $10M-$25M | 6-9x | Platform value, PE-attractive, significant bench |
| $25M+ | 8-12x | Strategic value, brand equity, institutional clients |

**Example scenario:**
- Acquire a management consulting boutique ($2M revenue, $500K EBITDA) at 3.5x = $1.75M
- Acquire an IT consulting firm ($1.5M revenue, $375K EBITDA) at 3x = $1.125M
- Acquire an HR/organizational consulting firm ($1M revenue, $280K EBITDA) at 3x = $840K
- Total acquisition cost: $3.72M
- Combined EBITDA: $1.155M + $200K in synergies = $1.355M
- Combined entity value at 6x: $8.13M
- **Equity created: $4.41M**

### The Expertise Platform Thesis

Each acquired boutique brings deep expertise in a specific domain. The combined platform offers clients a breadth of capabilities that no individual boutique can match:

| Acquisition | Expertise | Client Need Served |
|------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Your firm | Strategy/management consulting | Strategic planning, growth strategy |
| Acquisition #1 | Technology/digital transformation | IT strategy, system selection, implementation oversight |
| Acquisition #2 | HR/organizational effectiveness | Talent strategy, org design, change management |
| Acquisition #3 | Finance/operations | Cost optimization, process improvement, M&A integration |

A mid-market CEO who previously needed to hire 4 different boutiques can now work with one platform. This creates:

1. **Larger engagements** — multi-workstream projects that boutiques couldn't staff
2. **Longer relationships** — one engagement leads to referrals across practice areas
3. **Higher rates** — platform credibility commands a premium over individual boutique pricing
4. **Better talent** — top consultants want to work at firms with diverse, interesting work

### Utilization as the Key Lever

Consulting profitability is driven by **utilization rate** — the percentage of consultant time that's billed to clients.

| Utilization Rate | What It Means | Impact on EBITDA |
|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 50-60% | Typical struggling boutique | Breakeven or slight profit |
| 65-70% | Healthy boutique | 15-20% EBITDA margins |
| 70-80% | Well-managed firm | 20-30% EBITDA margins |
| 80%+ | Optimized (may risk burnout) | 30%+ EBITDA margins |

A roll-up improves utilization across the portfolio by:
- **Sharing bench**: When Firm A's consultants are between projects, they can staff on Firm B's engagements
- **Cross-staffing**: Multi-practice engagements use consultants from across the platform
- **Reducing feast-or-famine**: Larger portfolio of clients = more consistent demand

Even a 5-10% improvement in average utilization across the combined entity can add hundreds of thousands to EBITDA.

## Finding and Evaluating Targets

### Where to Source Deals

**Partner networks**
- The consulting industry runs on relationships. The best deals come through people you know.
- Former colleagues who started their own firms
- Partners at firms you've collaborated with on joint engagements
- Industry contacts who know boutique firm founders considering retirement or exit

**Professional associations**
- Association of Management Consulting Firms (AMCF)
- Institute of Management Consultants (IMC)
- Industry-specific associations where consulting firm founders congregate

**LinkedIn signals**
- Partners posting about "next chapters," sabbaticals, or burnout
- Firms that have stopped publishing thought leadership (often a sign of disengagement)
- Senior consultants leaving a firm (may indicate instability that creates acquisition opportunity)

**M&A brokers specializing in professional services**
- SI Partners, Equiteq, and Barney specialize in consulting/professional services M&A
- These brokers have deal flow from firm founders who want a confidential process

**Client referrals**
- Clients who work with multiple consulting firms may introduce you to smaller firms they like but that lack scale

### Evaluation Criteria for Consulting Firms

| Criteria | What to Look For | Red Flags |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Partner dependency** | Partners have strong client relationships but have begun transitioning key accounts to senior managers | All relationships are held by 1-2 partners with no succession plan |
| **Utilization rates** | 65-75% average across the team | Below 55% (demand problem) or above 85% (burnout risk) |
| **Revenue per consultant** | $200K-$400K per consultant (varies by specialty) | Below $150K per consultant (pricing or utilization problem) |
| **Realization rate** | 85-95% of billed time actually collected | Below 80% (scoping or collections problem) |
| **Client diversity** | No client > 15-20% of revenue | Any client > 30% of revenue |
| **Team tenure** | Senior consultants have been with the firm 3+ years | High turnover, especially among senior people |
| **Thought leadership** | Published research, conference speaking, recognized expertise | No published content, no market visibility |
| **Contract backlog** | 3-6 months of signed work in the pipeline | No backlog — all revenue is next-month dependent |
| **Specialty clarity** | Clear positioning in 1-2 domains | "We do everything" generalist positioning |

### The People Assessment (Critical for Consulting)

In most agency acquisitions, you evaluate the business first and the people second. In consulting, **reverse the order**. The people ARE the business.

For every target, assess:

1. **Which partners/senior consultants hold which client relationships?** Map every major client to the specific person who owns that relationship.
2. **What happens if partner X leaves?** If losing one partner means losing 40% of revenue, you need deal protections (see below).
3. **What's the bench depth?** Are there senior managers or directors who could step into partner roles?
4. **What are the team's career aspirations?** Senior consultants who want to make partner are more likely to stay if the platform offers a partnership path.
5. **What's the culture?** Consulting firms have strong cultures. If your culture and theirs are incompatible, integration will fail regardless of the financial math.

## Deal Structuring for Consulting Roll-Ups

Consulting acquisitions require specific deal structures that address people risk.

### Non-Compete and Non-Solicitation Agreements

**Non-negotiable.** Every partner and senior consultant in an acquired firm must sign:

- **Non-compete**: 2-3 years, covering the firm's practice areas and geographic markets
- **Non-solicitation of clients**: 2-3 years minimum, preventing departing consultants from taking clients
- **Non-solicitation of employees**: 2-3 years, preventing partners from recruiting former colleagues

These are the single most important deal protections in a consulting acquisition.

### Earnouts Tied to Retention

Structure 30-50% of the purchase price as an earnout tied to:

- **Revenue retention**: 100% earnout paid if 85%+ of revenue is retained after 18 months
- **Key person retention**: Bonus payments for each year that key partners stay
- **Growth targets**: Additional payments if the acquired practice grows post-close

**Example**: $2M total price = $1.2M at close + $800K earnout (50% tied to revenue retention at 12 months, 50% tied to key partner retention at 24 months)

### Equity Rolls for Partners

Partners in boutique consulting firms are often entrepreneurial. They don't just want cash — they want upside. Offer them equity in the combined platform:

- **Convert 20-40% of their purchase price to platform equity**
- Partners become invested in the combined entity's success
- Aligns incentives for 3-5 years (typical vesting period)
- Especially powerful if you can articulate a credible path to a PE exit at higher multiples

### Partnership Structures

Many consulting firms are organized as partnerships (LLPs, PLLCs). This creates structural complexity:

- Partnership agreements may require unanimous consent for a sale
- Partners may have different equity stakes and different payout expectations
- Tax treatment differs from a standard LLC or C-corp acquisition
- Consult an M&A attorney specializing in professional services partnerships

## Integration Strategy: Decentralize First

Consulting firms require a different integration philosophy than most agency roll-ups. The decentralized model works better because the value is in the individual practices and their partner relationships.

### The Recommended Model: Federated Platform

```
Platform Holding Company
├── Strategy Practice (Acquisition #1 - maintains identity and client relationships)
├── Technology Practice (Acquisition #2 - maintains identity and client relationships)
├── Operations Practice (Acquisition #3 - maintains identity and client relationships)
└── Shared Platform Services
    ├── Finance & Accounting
    ├── HR & Recruiting
    ├── Legal & Compliance
    ├── Marketing & Business Development
    ├── Knowledge Management
    └── Technology Infrastructure
```

**Each practice retains:**
- Its market-facing brand (initially)
- Its client relationships and partner structure
- Its delivery methodology
- Day-to-day operational autonomy

**The platform provides:**
- Back-office shared services (25-40% cost savings)
- Cross-selling infrastructure and incentives
- Recruiting and talent development at scale
- A combined brand for larger opportunities
- Technology platforms (CRM, knowledge management, time tracking)

### Integration Playbook: The First 100 Days

**Days 1-30: Protect Relationships**
- Founders/partners personally introduce you to their top 10 clients
- Meet every consultant 1-on-1 to understand career goals
- Identify flight risks and address them immediately with retention packages
- Change NOTHING operationally — the first month is about trust-building

**Days 31-60: Build Connections**
- Host a combined team offsite — consulting firms value face time
- Map expertise across the combined platform: who knows what, who's worked with whom
- Identify 2-3 cross-staffing opportunities for current engagements
- Begin aligning time tracking, billing, and reporting systems

**Days 61-100: Create Leverage**
- Launch internal "expertise directory" so consultants can find colleagues across practices
- Introduce a cross-selling incentive program (10-15% referral fees on cross-practice revenue)
- Standardize proposal templates that showcase the full platform
- Win the first multi-practice engagement — this proves the thesis to everyone

### What NOT to Do in the First Year

1. **Don't rebrand the acquired firms.** Consultants sell their personal credibility AND their firm's brand. Changing the brand damages both.
2. **Don't impose rigid utilization targets.** Boutique firms often have different norms. Sudden pressure to bill 80% when the culture was 65% will drive people out.
3. **Don't eliminate partner autonomy.** Partners who ran their own firms will not accept becoming middle managers overnight. Define clear authority and decision rights.
4. **Don't merge compensation structures immediately.** Different firms pay differently. Harmonize over 18-24 months, not 90 days.
5. **Don't cut thought leadership investments.** Publishing, speaking, and research are how consulting firms generate demand. These budgets are marketing, not overhead.

## Building the Platform at Scale

### Knowledge Management

The most valuable synergy in a consulting roll-up isn't cost savings — it's **knowledge leverage**. Build systems that capture and share intellectual capital:

- **Engagement databases**: Searchable repository of past projects, methodologies, and deliverables
- **Expert directories**: Who on the platform has expertise in what industries, functions, and technologies?
- **Methodology libraries**: Standardized frameworks and tools that consultants across practices can use
- **Training programs**: Senior consultants train junior ones across practice areas, building bench depth

### The Cross-Selling Engine

Cross-selling is where the real revenue synergy lives. Build it deliberately:

| Mechanism | How It Works | Expected Impact |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Referral incentives** | 10-15% of first-year revenue for cross-practice referrals | 5-10% revenue lift in year 1 |
| **Joint proposals** | Multi-practice proposals for complex client needs | Wins engagements neither practice could have won alone |
| **Account planning** | Quarterly review of top 20 clients: what else do they need? | 15-25% increase in revenue per client over 2 years |
| **Combined thought leadership** | Cross-practice publications and events | Positions the platform as a thought leader, generates new inquiries |

### Talent Development as a Competitive Advantage

Boutique consulting firms struggle to develop talent because they lack:
- Formal training programs
- Clear career paths (partner or nothing)
- Exposure to diverse industries and problem types
- Mentorship from a broad set of senior leaders

The platform solves all of this. Build:

- **Structured career paths**: Analyst → Consultant → Senior Consultant → Manager → Director → Partner
- **Rotation programs**: Junior consultants rotate across practices for 6-12 months
- **Training academies**: Quarterly training on consulting skills, industry knowledge, and platform methodologies
- **Mentorship matching**: Senior consultants from different practices mentor junior staff

Better talent development = lower turnover = higher margins = higher valuation.

### Revenue Diversification

Expand beyond traditional project consulting:

| Revenue Stream | Description | Margin | Recurring? |
|---------------|-------------|--------|-----------|
| **Project consulting** | Traditional engagements | 40-55% | No |
| **Advisory retainers** | Monthly access to senior partners | 70-80% | Yes |
| **Fractional executives** | Part-time CMO, CFO, CTO placements | 50-65% | Yes (6-12 month) |
| **Training and workshops** | Client training on methodologies | 60-75% | Semi-recurring |
| **Subscription research** | Industry benchmarks and insights | 80-90% | Yes |
| **Implementation support** | Post-strategy execution assistance | 45-55% | Engagement-based |

Shifting 20-30% of revenue to advisory retainers and fractional roles significantly improves the predictability (and valuation) of the combined entity.

## PE and Investor Considerations

### What PE Firms Evaluate in Consulting Platforms

- **Revenue per consultant**: $250K+ is healthy, $350K+ is excellent
- **Utilization rates**: 70-80% target
- **Realization rates**: 85-95%
- **Key person risk**: Can the business function if any single partner leaves?
- **Organic growth rate**: 10-20%+ on top of acquisitive growth
- **Client retention**: 80-90%+ annual revenue retention
- **Backlog**: 3-6 months of contracted work
- **Management depth**: A team that runs the firm, not just a founder and partners

### Typical PE Terms for Consulting Roll-Ups

- **Valuation**: 6-10x EBITDA for platforms with $2M+ EBITDA
- **Equity split**: PE takes 55-75%, management retains 25-45% (consulting platforms often retain more because the partners ARE the value)
- **Hold period**: 3-5 years
- **Acquisition funding**: PE commits capital for 5-10 add-on acquisitions
- **Management incentives**: Equity appreciation, carried interest on exits, annual performance bonuses

### The Path to Exit

| Exit Route | Typical Multiple | Best For |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Sale to larger PE firm | 8-12x EBITDA | Platforms with $5M+ EBITDA and growth trajectory |
| Sale to strategic buyer | 7-10x EBITDA | Platforms with niche expertise a larger firm wants |
| Management buyout | 5-7x EBITDA | Partners who want to regain full ownership |
| IPO (rare for this size) | 10-15x EBITDA | Platforms with $50M+ revenue and a growth story |

## Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| **Partner departure** | Client loss, revenue decline, team destabilization | Non-competes, earnouts tied to retention, equity rolls, clear career paths |
| **Client follows the person, not the firm** | Revenue evaporates if key consultants leave | Gradually introduce clients to multiple team members, build institutional relationships |
| **Utilization decline post-acquisition** | Margins compress, EBITDA targets missed | Shared bench across practices, cross-staffing, active pipeline management |
| **Culture clash** | Talent turnover, productivity decline | Federated model preserves practice cultures, gradual harmonization |
| **Scope creep in integration** | Trying to standardize everything at once overwhelms teams | Prioritize back-office consolidation first, leave client delivery alone initially |
| **Compensation harmonization** | Partners from different firms with different pay expectations | Transparent framework, transition over 18-24 months, tie increases to platform performance |
| **Market cyclicality** | Consulting is discretionary spending — first to be cut in downturns | Diversify across industries, build recurring advisory revenue, maintain cash reserves |

## The Roll-Up Timeline

**Months 1-6: Thesis and Targeting**
- Define your platform vision: What practices? What industries? What geography?
- Build relationships with 15-20 potential targets
- Develop the financial model and financing approach
- Engage an M&A attorney familiar with professional services

**Months 6-12: First Acquisition**
- Close deal #1 — prioritize a firm with complementary expertise and cultural compatibility
- Spend 90 days building trust and relationships
- Start shared services integration (back-office only)
- Prove the cross-selling thesis with 1-2 wins

**Months 12-24: Build the Platform**
- Close deals #2 and #3
- Build the shared services infrastructure
- Launch knowledge management and cross-selling programs
- Hire a COO or Chief of Staff to manage integration

**Months 24-36: Professionalize**
- Combined revenue approaching $10M+
- Formalized career paths, training programs, and talent development
- Advisory and fractional revenue growing as a percentage of total
- Consider PE partnership to accelerate further acquisitions

**Months 36-60: Scale and Position for Exit**
- Platform revenue at $15-30M+ with 25-30% EBITDA margins
- 5-8 practice areas, 100+ consultants
- Strong organic growth on top of acquisitive growth
- Valued at 7-10x EBITDA — multiple arbitrage fully realized

## FAQ

**What makes consulting roll-ups riskier than other agency roll-ups?**

People dependency. In an SEO agency, the processes and tools contribute significantly to client outcomes. In consulting, the individual consultant's expertise and relationships are the product. When key people leave a consulting firm, clients often follow. This is why non-competes, earnouts, and equity rolls are essential in consulting acquisitions.

**How do I prevent partners from leaving after I acquire their firm?**

Three mechanisms, used together: (1) Earnouts that tie 30-50% of the purchase price to their staying for 2-3 years. (2) Equity in the combined platform that vests over 3-5 years. (3) A genuinely better professional environment — more resources, bigger engagements, less administrative burden, a clear path to building something larger. If you can only offer money but not a better professional experience, partners will leave when the earnout ends.

**Should I acquire firms in the same specialty or different ones?**

Different. The value of a consulting roll-up comes from building a multi-practice platform. Acquiring three strategy firms creates size but limited synergy. Acquiring a strategy firm, a technology firm, and an operations firm creates a platform that can serve clients across multiple needs — and each acquisition opens cross-selling opportunities.

**What utilization rate should I target for the combined platform?**

70-75% is a healthy target. Below 65% and margins compress. Above 80% and you're at burnout risk, which drives turnover. The platform's advantage is that shared bench capacity smooths utilization across practices — when one practice is slow, its consultants can support another practice.

**How do I handle different compensation models across acquired firms?**

Slowly and transparently. In year 1, maintain each firm's existing compensation structure. In year 2, introduce a unified framework with clear tiers, but grandfather existing compensation for 12-18 months. The framework should reward utilization, client development, and platform contribution (cross-selling, mentorship, thought leadership). Never surprise partners with compensation changes.

**When is the right time to bring in PE?**

After 2-3 acquisitions, when you've proven that: (1) your integration model works, (2) cross-selling generates real revenue, (3) key partners have stayed, and (4) the platform's organic growth rate is healthy. PE firms want to see a proven playbook they can fund at scale — not a thesis they need to help you prove.
