# B2B Influencer Posts: Agency Exits & Acquisitions

20 high-performing social media post concepts targeting three audiences: entrepreneurs running $3M+ agencies, agency founders considering a sale, and investors acquiring marketing agencies. Each post uses a proven B2B hook format designed for LinkedIn and Twitter/X.

Full scripts for all 20 posts are available in the `scripts/` directory. Each script includes a LinkedIn version (1,200-1,500 characters) and a Twitter/X thread (8 tweets).

---

## Audience Definitions

| Audience | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Entrepreneur** | Agency or services founder above $3M revenue, scaling operations |
| **Seller** | Agency founder actively exploring or preparing for an exit |
| **Investor** | PE firm, holding company, or operator acquiring marketing agencies |

## The 20 Posts

### Post 1 — Your Agency Isn't Worth What Your Broker Says

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** Contrarian
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** Your agency isn't worth 6x revenue. Stop listening to brokers who tell you it is.

**Key points:**
- Revenue multiples are misleading — buyers pay on adjusted EBITDA
- Most agencies under $10M trade between 4-8x EBITDA, not revenue
- Brokers inflate projections to win your listing
- Walkthrough of how a $5M revenue agency with 15% margins is actually a $3M valuation, not $30M
- How to calculate a realistic number before you talk to anyone

**CTA:** What's the most surprising valuation reality you've encountered?

### Post 2 — Why Most Agency Acquisitions Fail

**Audience:** Investor
**Hook type:** Data/stat
**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Hook:** Most agency acquisitions fail within 24 months. Here's what the successful ones do differently.

**Key points:**
- The #1 killer: losing key client relationships during transition
- Successful acquirers keep the founder for 12-18 months minimum
- Culture integration matters more than financial engineering
- Three-phase integration playbook: stabilize (months 1-3), optimize (months 4-9), scale (months 10-18)
- The operators who win treat acquired agencies like partnerships, not subsidiaries

**CTA:** What's the biggest integration mistake you've seen?

### Post 3 — The Founder Who Turned Down $8M

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** Story
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** An agency founder turned down an $8M offer. 18 months later, he sold for $4M. Here's what went wrong.

**Key points:**
- He believed growth would continue at 40% YoY — it didn't
- Lost two anchor clients that represented 35% of revenue
- The original buyer moved on to a competitor
- Market conditions shifted — multiples compressed
- Lesson: a good offer today beats a perfect offer that never comes
- How to evaluate whether an offer is "good enough"

**CTA:** Have you ever regretted not taking a deal when it was on the table?

**Hook type:** Numbered list
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Key points:**
1. Every major client has your personal cell phone number
2. You approve every proposal before it goes out
3. No one else can sell new business
4. Your top talent would leave if you stepped back
5. You haven't taken more than 5 consecutive days off in 2+ years

- Each sign includes a one-line fix
- Close with: Fixing even 3 of these doubles your enterprise value

**CTA:** How many of these hit home? Be honest.

**Hook type:** Pain point
**Platform:** LinkedIn

### Post 7 — The 90-Day Disappearance Test

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** Question
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** If you disappeared for 90 days, would your agency survive? If the answer is no, you don't have a sellable business.

**Key points:**
- Buyers stress-test founder dependency before anything else
- The three areas they probe: client relationships, sales pipeline, operational decision-making
- How to run a self-audit: delegate everything for 2 weeks and see what breaks
- The things that break reveal exactly what to fix before going to market
- Real example: a founder who spent 12 months removing himself before a successful exit

**CTA:** Try it for 2 weeks. What breaks first tells you everything.

### Post 8 — Stop Chasing $10M Revenue

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Hook type:** "Stop doing X"
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** Stop chasing $10M in revenue. A $4M agency with 30% margins is worth more than a $10M agency with 8%.

**Key points:**
- Revenue is vanity, EBITDA is sanity, cash flow is reality
- A $4M agency at 30% margins = $1.2M EBITDA = $6-9.6M valuation
- A $10M agency at 8% margins = $800K EBITDA = $4-6.4M valuation
- Why founders chase revenue: ego, wrong advisors, misunderstanding of value
- The high-margin playbook: fire low-margin clients, raise prices, productize delivery
- Doing less, better, is the fastest path to a valuable agency

**CTA:** What's your actual EBITDA margin? Most founders don't know.

**Hook type:** Confession/hindsight
**Platform:** LinkedIn

- Year 2 was fixing financials that should have been clean from day one
- Year 3 was actually going to market — which should have been month 6
- The 90-day sprint: clean financials, document processes, diversify revenue, hire a second-in-command
- Most sellers over-prepare on the wrong things and under-prepare on what buyers actually care about
- If you're thinking about selling in 2+ years, start today

**CTA:** What's the one thing holding you back from starting your exit prep?

### Post 10 — We Bought a $3M Agency and Doubled It in 18 Months

**Audience:** Investor
**Hook type:** Big outcome
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** We bought a $3M agency, removed the founder from delivery in 6 months, and doubled revenue in 18. Here's exactly how.

**Key points:**
- Step 1: Kept the founder but moved them to business development only
- Step 2: Promoted the best senior manager to Head of Delivery
- Step 3: Standardized SOPs and created a real project management layer
- Step 4: Cross-sold services into the acquirer's existing client base
- Step 5: Invested in outbound sales for the first time — the founder had grown purely on referrals
- The math: $180K investment in ops and sales hires yielded $3M in new revenue

**CTA:** What would you do differently in an agency acquisition?

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Hook type:** Contrarian
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** The worst thing that happened to your agency was crossing $3M. Here's what I mean.

**Key points:**
- At $1-3M, the founder does everything and it works
- At $3M+, that same approach becomes the ceiling
- Revenue grows but profit doesn't — you're hiring to keep up, not to build leverage
- The $3-5M "no man's land": too big to be scrappy, too small to have real infrastructure
- Three shifts required: from doer to manager, from generalist to specialist services, from reactive to systematized
- The founders who break through $5M are the ones who rebuild the business at $3M

**CTA:** If you're stuck between $3-5M, what's the thing that feels hardest to change?

### Post 12 — Client Concentration Will Kill Your Valuation

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** Data-backed warning
**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Hook:** Agencies with 80%+ revenue from 3 or fewer clients sell for 40-60% less. Here's how to fix client concentration before you go to market.

**Key points:**
- Buyers see concentrated revenue as existential risk — they discount accordingly
- The safe threshold: no single client above 20%, top 5 clients under 50%
- Why founders tolerate concentration: it's comfortable and profitable in the short term
- The fix (12-month plan): raise prices on big clients, aggressively pursue mid-market accounts, build a repeatable sales motion
- A diversified book at $4M is worth more than a concentrated book at $6M
- Practical framework for measuring and reducing concentration quarter by quarter

**CTA:** What % of your revenue comes from your top 3 clients? Drop the number below.

**Audience:** Investor
**Hook type:** Story + authority
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** I've looked at 200+ agency deals in the last 3 years. The #1 red flag has nothing to do with financials.

**Key points:**
- The red flag: the founder can't clearly explain what the agency does in one sentence
- If the founder can't articulate it, clients are buying the founder, not the service
- This signals: no positioning, no differentiation, no repeatable delivery model
- The agencies that sell well have a clear, narrow identity
- "Full-service" is a red flag — it means "we'll do whatever you pay us for"
- What buyers want: "We do X for Y and our process is Z"

**CTA:** Can you explain your agency in one sentence?

### Post 14 — What PE Firms Evaluate in the First 48 Hours

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** Numbered list / insider knowledge
**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Hook:** 5 things every PE firm evaluates in the first 48 hours of looking at your agency.

**Key points:**
1. **Revenue retention rate** — Are clients staying? Net revenue retention above 100% is gold
2. **EBITDA margins and trend** — Not just current margin, but direction over 3 years
3. **Founder dependency** — Can this business run without the founder in 12 months?
4. **Client concentration** — Any single client above 25% of revenue is a problem
5. **Team depth** — Is there a management layer, or does everything run through one person?

- For each: what "good" looks like vs. what gets your CIM passed on immediately
- How to self-audit against these five criteria today

**CTA:** Grade yourself 1-5 on each. Where do you score lowest?

### Post 15 — Growth Feels Like a Trap

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Hook type:** Pain point / empathy
**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Hook:** Your agency hit $3M. Your best people are burning out. Clients keep escalating to you. Growth feels like a trap. Sound familiar?

**Key points:**
- The $3-5M growth trap: every new dollar of revenue feels harder than the last
- You're solving yesterday's problems with yesterday's team structure
- The burnout cycle: overwork → turnover → more pressure on founder → more overwork
- Breaking the cycle requires uncomfortable decisions: firing clients, raising prices, hiring ahead of revenue
- The agencies that get through this phase share one trait: the founder makes the business smaller before making it bigger
- Practical first step: identify your 3 lowest-margin clients and plan to replace them

**CTA:** What would you change first if you could rebuild your agency from scratch?

### Post 18 — Stop Trying to Grow Your Way to a Better Valuation

**Audience:** Seller
**Hook type:** "Stop doing X"
**Platform:** LinkedIn

**Hook:** Stop trying to grow your way to a better valuation. Buyers don't pay more for top-line growth. They pay for predictability.

**Key points:**
- The instinct: "If I get to $7M, I'll get a better multiple" — usually wrong
- Growth fueled by low-margin project work actually decreases value
- What buyers pay premium multiples for: recurring revenue, process maturity, diversified clients, management depth
- A $4M agency with monthly retainers, documented delivery, and a leadership team commands higher multiples than a $7M agency growing 30% on one-off projects
- Practical: focus the next 12 months on margin, retention, and systems — not new revenue
- The paradox of selling: the best exits come from founders who stop optimizing for exit

**CTA:** What's one thing you could fix in your agency that has nothing to do with revenue?

### Post 19 — I Needed More Clients (I Was Wrong)

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Hook type:** Confession / lesson learned
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** At $3M, I thought I needed more clients. At $7M, I realized I needed fewer clients who paid more. That single shift changed everything.

**Key points:**
- More clients = more complexity, more management overhead, more diluted service
- Fewer, larger clients = deeper relationships, higher retention, better margins
- The math: 10 clients at $50K/month vs. 50 clients at $10K/month — same revenue, completely different business
- How to move upmarket: raise minimum engagement size, invest in case studies, develop senior talent
- The hardest part: saying no to small deals when you still need revenue
- Practical transition: raise prices 20% for all new clients, build a case study that justifies premium positioning

**CTA:** What's your average client value? If it's under $20K/month, there's a simpler business waiting for you.

### Post 20 — The 4-Agency Roll-Up That Sold for $42M

**Audience:** Investor
**Hook type:** Big outcome / playbook
**Platform:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

**Hook:** A holding company rolled up 4 agencies doing $2-4M each into a combined entity that sold for $42M. Here's the playbook behind every step.

**Key points:**
- Step 1: Acquired 4 complementary agencies (SEO, paid media, creative, CRO) over 18 months
- Step 2: Unified back-office (finance, HR, legal) immediately — kept delivery teams separate
- Step 3: Cross-sold services into each agency's existing client base — avg deal size increased 3x
- Step 4: Built a single brand story and go-to-market motion for the combined entity
- Step 5: Achieved $12M combined revenue at 28% EBITDA margin within 24 months
- Step 6: Sold to a larger PE firm at 3.5x the total acquisition cost
- Why it worked: complementary services, minimal overlap, and shared ICP
- What could go wrong: culture clashes, client churn during transition, integration debt

**CTA:** What combination of agency specialties would you roll up?
