# Post 19 — I Needed More Clients (I Was Wrong)

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

At $3M, I thought I needed more clients. At $7M, I realized I needed fewer clients who paid more. That single shift changed everything.

When I was running a $3M agency, my dashboard showed 47 active clients. I wore it like a badge of honor. "Look at all this demand." Every Monday started with a status call marathon. Every Friday ended with my ops team triaging which fires to put out first.

We were growing. But we were drowning.

Here's what 47 clients at an average of ~$5,300/month actually looks like:

- 4 project managers stretched across 12 accounts each
- Junior talent doing senior work because you can't afford senior people on $5K/month engagements
- Client churn at 30%+ annually because nobody feels like a priority
- Your best strategists spending 60% of their time on internal coordination, not client work

By the time we hit $7M, we'd cut our client count to 14. Average engagement: $41,600/month. Same revenue band. Completely different business.

The math was obvious in hindsight. But the psychology was brutal.

Saying no to a $6K/month deal when you're trying to make payroll feels insane. Raising your minimum engagement from $5K to $25K means turning away 80% of inbound. Your sales team will panic. Your pipeline will look empty for 60-90 days.

But here's what happens on the other side:

- Retention jumps to 85%+ because each client gets your A-team
- Margins improve 8-12 points because senior talent is leveraged, not diluted
- Your team's quality of life transforms — no more context-switching across 12 accounts
- Referrals from $40K/month clients bring in other $40K/month clients

How to make the shift:

1. Raise prices 20% for all new business immediately — not existing clients, just new ones
2. Build one premium case study that justifies why you're worth $30K+/month
3. Invest in 2-3 senior hires who can own client relationships end-to-end
4. Set a minimum engagement size and enforce it — no exceptions for 6 months
5. Fire your bottom 20% of clients by revenue (they're consuming 40% of your capacity)

The hardest part isn't the strategy. It's watching deals walk away in the short term while you trust the math to work in the long term.

What's your average client value? If it's under $20K/month, there's a simpler, more profitable business waiting for you on the other side of that number.
