# Post 17 — $2M Agency With Great Ops vs. $8M Agency With a Hero Founder

**Audience:** Investor
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

Would you rather buy a $2M agency with 35% margins and a strong ops team, or an $8M agency where the founder IS the business? The answer reveals everything about how you think about acquisitions.

I've seen both deals play out dozens of times. And the pattern is almost always the same.

The $2M agency looks underwhelming on a spreadsheet. Lower revenue. Smaller team. Less brand recognition. But under the hood, it's a machine. Processes are documented. Client relationships sit with account directors, not the founder. The P&L is clean. Margins are healthy because the business was built to run efficiently — not to impress anyone.

The $8M agency looks incredible in a pitch deck. Strong revenue. Big logos. A charismatic founder who commands the room. But pull back the curtain and you find the founder is the top salesperson, the lead strategist, and the primary relationship holder for 60% of the revenue. Remove them and the business doesn't shrink — it collapses.

Here's what actually happens post-acquisition:

The $2M agency with strong ops? You inject growth capital, layer in a sales team, and expand the service offering. Within 24 months, it's a $6M business running at 30% margins. Your total return is 3-4x in under 3 years.

The $8M agency with the hero founder? The founder hits their earnout targets (barely), checks out emotionally at month 14, and starts their next venture. Three key clients leave because their "guy" isn't running things anymore. By month 18, you're looking at a $3M agency that you paid $8M multiples for.

I've watched first-time acquirers make this mistake over and over. They chase the bigger number because it feels safer. "We're buying a proven business." But what they're actually buying is a personal brand with an org chart attached.

The question isn't which agency has more revenue. It's which agency has more enterprise value independent of any single person.

Which would you choose? The answer says more about your strategy than any deck.
