# Post 20 — The 4-Agency Roll-Up That Sold for $42M

**Audience:** Investor
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

A holding company rolled up 4 agencies doing $2-4M each into a combined entity that sold for $42M. Here's the playbook behind every step.

I had a front-row seat to this deal over 3 years. It's the cleanest roll-up execution I've seen in the agency space, and it followed a logic that most acquirers overcomplicate.

The thesis was simple: find 4 agencies with complementary services, a shared ideal client profile, and founders who wanted liquidity but weren't ready to fully exit. Then combine them into something worth more than the sum of its parts.

Here's how it played out:

Step 1: Acquired 4 agencies over 18 months — an SEO shop ($3.2M revenue), a paid media agency ($2.8M), a creative studio ($2.1M), and a CRO consultancy ($2.4M). Total acquisition cost: ~$12M across all four deals. Each founder stayed on with a 2-year earnout tied to their division's performance.

Step 2: Unified back-office immediately. Finance, HR, legal, and ops were centralized within the first 90 days. This alone saved $1.4M annually. But delivery teams stayed separate. The SEO team kept doing SEO. The creative team kept doing creative. No forced integration of the work product.

Step 3: Cross-sold services into each agency's existing client base. The SEO agency's clients needed paid media. The creative studio's clients needed CRO. Average deal size increased from $14K/month to $43K/month within 12 months. This wasn't theoretical synergy — it was real revenue from warm relationships.

Step 4: Built a unified brand and go-to-market motion. One website. One sales team. One pitch that told the story of integrated digital performance. New business pipeline grew 4x because they could now compete for $500K+ annual contracts that no single agency could have won alone.

Step 5: Hit $12M combined revenue at 28% EBITDA margin within 24 months of the first acquisition. The margin improvement came from shared overhead, cross-sell efficiency, and pricing power that none of the individual agencies had.

Step 6: Sold to a mid-market PE firm for $42M — approximately 3.5x the total acquisition cost. The buyer was paying for scale, margin quality, and a diversified service platform they didn't have to build from scratch.

Why it worked: complementary services with minimal client overlap, a shared ICP (mid-market B2B SaaS), and founders who were operators, not just figureheads.

What could go wrong (and almost did): culture clashes between the creative team and the performance teams took 6 months to resolve. Two enterprise clients churned during the brand transition. And the integration of four different tech stacks created 8 months of operational debt that nearly broke the ops team.

This model is repeatable. But only if you get the sequencing right: acquire, stabilize, cross-sell, unify, scale, exit.

What combination of agency specialties would you roll up?
