# Post 10 — We Bought a $3M Agency and Doubled It in 18 Months

**Audience:** Investor
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

We bought a $3M agency, removed the founder from delivery in 6 months, and doubled revenue in 18. Here's exactly how.

When we closed the acquisition in Q2 2024, the agency was doing $3.1M in annual revenue with 22% EBITDA margins. Good, not great. But we saw something the seller didn't realize they had: an incredibly skilled delivery team that had been held back by a founder who couldn't let go.

The founder was involved in every client call, approved every deliverable, and personally managed the top 5 accounts. Sound familiar? The team was capable, but they'd never been given permission to lead.

Here's the exact playbook we ran:

**Step 1: Keep the founder, but move them to business development only.**
We didn't push the founder out. We repositioned them. Within 60 days of close, we transitioned the founder out of all delivery responsibilities and into a pure BD role — leveraging their relationships and reputation to open doors. They stayed on for 15 months total. It was the best use of their talent and it freed the entire ops team to operate.

**Step 2: Promote the best senior manager to Head of Delivery.**
The agency had a senior project manager who'd been there four years. She knew the clients, she knew the work, and she'd been quietly running operations in the founder's shadow for years. We promoted her to Head of Delivery, gave her real authority and a meaningful equity stake, and got out of her way. Within 90 days, client satisfaction scores actually went up.

**Step 3: Standardize SOPs and create a real project management layer.**
The agency had been running on tribal knowledge and Slack threads. We invested $35K in documenting every core workflow, implementing a proper PM tool, and creating repeatable playbooks for onboarding, delivery, and reporting. This one investment cut delivery time on standard engagements by 20% and freed up capacity for new business.

**Step 4: Cross-sell services into the acquirer's existing client base.**
Our holding company had a portfolio of complementary businesses. Within 6 months, we introduced the acquired agency's services to 14 existing clients across the portfolio. Eight converted. That was $1.1M in new annual revenue with zero customer acquisition cost.

**Step 5: Invest in outbound sales for the first time.**
The founder had grown the agency entirely through referrals and word-of-mouth. That's great for getting to $3M, but it's a ceiling without outbound. We hired a sales development rep and a closer, built a targeted outbound sequence, and focused on mid-market companies in the verticals where the agency had proven case studies. Within 9 months, outbound generated $1.9M in new pipeline and closed $900K.

**The math:** We invested roughly $180K in operations upgrades and sales hires. That investment yielded approximately $3M in new annualized revenue within 18 months. The agency went from $3.1M to $6.2M in revenue with margins expanding from 22% to 28% as the SOP improvements created operating leverage.

The agency was never broken. It was underleveraged. The founder had built something real — they just didn't know how to scale it past themselves.

What would you do differently in an agency acquisition? I'm always refining the playbook.
