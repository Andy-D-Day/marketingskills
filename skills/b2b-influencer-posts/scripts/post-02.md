# Post 2 — Why Most Agency Acquisitions Fail

**Audience:** Investor
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

Most agency acquisitions fail within 24 months. Here's what the successful ones do differently.

I've watched private equity firms buy $4M agencies at 6x EBITDA and destroy them in under two years. And I've watched solo operators acquire the same size agency with an SBA loan and 3x their investment in 36 months.

The difference is never the financial model. It's always the integration.

The #1 killer of agency acquisitions is losing key client relationships during the transition. Clients hired the founder. They trust the founder. The moment they sense the founder is checked out or gone, they start taking calls from competitors.

I watched a PE-backed roll-up lose 40% of an acquired agency's revenue in 9 months because they pushed the founder out at close. That $6M acquisition became a $2M write-down.

Here's what the successful acquirers do instead:

They keep the founder for 12-18 months minimum. Not as a figurehead — as the active face of client relationships while trust transfers to the new team.

They treat culture integration as seriously as financial integration. Agency people are not SaaS employees. They chose the agency for its culture. Blow that up and your best talent walks within 6 months.

And they follow a three-phase playbook:

**Months 1-3: Stabilize.** Change nothing. Learn everything. Meet every client. Understand every process before you touch it.

**Months 4-9: Optimize.** Introduce systems gradually. Upgrade the tech stack. Start cross-selling across the portfolio. But only after you've earned trust.

**Months 10-18: Scale.** Now you can restructure, hire, expand service lines, and push growth.

The operators who consistently win at agency M&A treat acquired businesses like partnerships, not subsidiaries. They respect what was built before they showed up.

Skip the phases, lose the people. Lose the people, lose the clients. Lose the clients, lose the deal.

What's the biggest integration mistake you've seen?
