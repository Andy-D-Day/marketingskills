# Post 18 — Stop Trying to Grow Your Way to a Better Valuation

**Audience:** Seller
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

Stop trying to grow your way to a better valuation. Buyers don't pay more for top-line growth. They pay for predictability.

I hear this from agency founders every month: "If I can just get to $7M, I'll get a better multiple." And almost every time, it's wrong.

Here's why.

The growth from $4M to $7M usually comes from the easiest revenue to close — one-off projects, scope expansions you'll never repeat, or a big client that represents 30% of your new run rate. You grew the top line, but you made the business less valuable in the process.

Buyers see through this instantly.

A $4M agency with 80% retainer revenue, documented delivery playbooks, and a leadership team that runs client relationships without the founder will trade at 6-7x EBITDA. That's a $4M agency valued at $4.2-$4.9M on $700K EBITDA.

A $7M agency growing 30% year-over-year on project work, with the founder managing the top 5 accounts, and margins compressing because you hired fast to service new work? That's trading at 3.5-4.5x. On $560K EBITDA (8% margins from the growth chaos), you're looking at $2-$2.5M.

Read that again. The $4M agency is worth more than the $7M agency.

This is the math that breaks founders' brains. But it's the math that every buyer runs.

What buyers actually pay premium multiples for:

1. Recurring revenue — monthly retainers with 12+ month average tenure
2. Process maturity — delivery that doesn't depend on tribal knowledge
3. Client diversification — no single client above 15% of revenue
4. Management depth — a team that makes decisions without the founder

If you're 12 months from a potential exit, stop chasing new logos. Focus on margin improvement, client retention, and building systems. Convert project clients to retainers. Document your delivery methodology. Promote your best people into client-facing leadership roles.

The paradox of selling an agency: the best exits come from founders who stop optimizing for exit and start optimizing for a business they'd want to own forever.

What's one thing you could fix in your agency that has nothing to do with revenue?
