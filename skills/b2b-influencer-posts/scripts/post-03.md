# Post 3 — The Founder Who Turned Down $8M

**Audience:** Seller
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

An agency founder turned down an $8M offer. 18 months later, he sold for $4M. Here's what went wrong.

I got a call from a friend in late 2022. His digital agency had just hit $6M in revenue, growing 40% year over year. A strategic buyer came in with a strong offer — $8M, clean terms, 60% at close, 40% over a two-year earn-out.

He turned it down.

His logic made sense at the time. If he was growing at 40%, he'd be at $8.5M revenue in a year. At a better multiple, maybe he could get $12-15M. Why sell now?

Here's what actually happened:

Within six months, two anchor clients — representing 35% of combined revenue — pulled their accounts. One went in-house. One got acquired and the new parent company had an agency of record.

Revenue dropped from $6M to $4.2M. Growth went negative. His best account director left for a competitor and took a mid-size client with her.

The original buyer? They'd already moved on. Acquired a competitor of his for $5M.

By the time he went back to market, multiples had compressed across the sector. Fewer buyers were writing checks. Due diligence got harder because his financials showed a declining trajectory.

He sold 18 months later for $4M, with worse terms — 40% at close, 60% tied to an earn-out based on revenue targets he wasn't confident he could hit.

The lesson is brutal but simple: a good offer today beats a perfect offer that never comes.

Here's how to evaluate whether an offer is "good enough":

1. Does the cash at close cover your personal financial goals?
2. Can you live with the earn-out terms even in a downside scenario?
3. Is your business trending up right now — and can you guarantee it will be in 12 months?
4. Have you stress-tested what happens if you lose your top two clients?

If you're sitting on an offer and waiting for something better, ask yourself honestly: what specific, concrete thing will change in 12 months that makes waiting worth the risk?

If you can't answer that clearly, you probably have your answer.

Have you ever regretted not taking a deal when it was on the table?
