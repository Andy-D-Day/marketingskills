# Post 7 — The 90-Day Disappearance Test

**Audience:** Seller
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

If you disappeared for 90 days, would your agency survive? If the answer is no, you don't have a sellable business.

I say this to agency founders all the time, and it makes them uncomfortable. Good. It should.

Here's the reality: buyers stress-test founder dependency before they look at your revenue, your margins, or your client list. Because none of those numbers matter if they all walk out the door the day you do.

There are three areas every serious buyer probes during diligence:

**Client relationships.** Who holds the key relationships — you or your team? If you're the one clients call when something goes sideways, that's a problem. Buyers see it and immediately discount your multiple by 1-2x.

**Sales pipeline.** If new business only comes through your personal network and referrals, you don't have a sales engine — you have a founder with a Rolodex. Buyers want a repeatable system that generates pipeline without you.

**Operational decision-making.** Who decides which projects get prioritized? Who approves the creative? Who resolves team conflicts? If every answer starts with "I," you have a job, not a business.

Here's what I tell every founder who's 12-24 months from a potential exit: run a self-audit. Delegate everything — and I mean everything — for two weeks. Step out of client calls. Don't touch the pipeline. Let your team make every decision.

Then watch what breaks.

I worked with a founder last year who did exactly this. Within three days, two clients escalated because nobody knew how to handle scope creep without him. The pipeline went cold because no one was following up on inbound leads. And his ops manager froze on a staffing decision she'd never been empowered to make.

Those breakdowns told him precisely what to fix. He spent the next 12 months building out a client success team, hiring a business development lead, and training his ops manager to run autonomously. When he went to market, the buyer's diligence confirmed what the founder already knew — the business ran without him.

He closed at 7.2x EBITDA. If he'd gone to market 12 months earlier with all those dependencies intact, he would have been lucky to get 4x.

Try the disappearance test for 2 weeks. What breaks first tells you everything you need to know about your exit readiness.
