# Post 14 — What PE Firms Evaluate in the First 48 Hours

**Audience:** Seller
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

5 things every PE firm evaluates in the first 48 hours of looking at your agency.

PE associates look at dozens of deals per quarter. Most get passed on within 48 hours. Here's what decides your fate.

**1. Revenue retention rate.** Net revenue retention above 100% is gold — it means clients spend more each year, not just renew. An agency with 92% gross retention and 115% NRR gets a meeting. One with 85% gross and 95% NRR gets filed away.

**2. EBITDA margins and trend.** Direction matters more than the number. 18% margin trending up from 12% beats 22% trending down from 28%. They want three years of financials moving the right direction.

**3. Founder dependency.** The real question: "If this founder leaves at month 13, do we still have a business?" If you're the lead strategist, primary seller, and final approver — they see a job, not an asset.

**4. Client concentration.** Any client above 25% of revenue gets flagged. Top 3 above 50% is serious. They stress-test your biggest client leaving at month 6 post-close.

**5. Team depth.** They want department heads who own P&Ls, a COO who runs operations, and senior leads who hold client relationships independently. Flat org chart = single point of failure.

Self-audit today: grade yourself 1-5 on each. Anything below 3 needs 12-18 months to fix.

Grade yourself 1-5 on each. Where do you score lowest?

## Twitter/X Thread

**Tweet 1 (Hook):**
5 things every PE firm evaluates in the first 48 hours of looking at your agency.

Most deals get passed on before the end of day two. Here's what they're looking at:

**Tweet 2:**
1. Revenue retention rate.

They want net revenue retention (NRR) above 100%.

That means existing clients are spending MORE each year — not just renewing.

85% gross retention with 95% NRR = red flag.
92% gross retention with 115% NRR = second meeting immediately.

**Tweet 3:**
2. EBITDA margins and trend.

The number matters. The direction matters more.

18% margin trending UP from 12%? Growth story.
22% margin trending DOWN from 28%? Decay story.

They'll take the first one every time. Three years of clean financials trending the right way.

**Tweet 4:**
3. Founder dependency.

The real question: "If this founder leaves at month 13, do we still have a business?"

If you're the lead strategist, primary sales channel, and final approver on all work — they see a business that buys them a job, not an asset.

**Tweet 5:**
4. Client concentration.

Any single client above 25% of revenue = immediate flag.
Top 3 clients above 50% = serious concern.

They stress-test: what happens if your biggest client leaves 6 months post-close?

If the answer is "margins collapse," the deal dies or terms shift hard against you.

**Tweet 6:**
5. Team depth.

They want to see:
- Department heads who own their P&L
- A second-in-command who can run operations
- Senior client leads who hold relationships independently

If the org chart is flat with everyone reporting to the founder, that's a single point of failure.

**Tweet 7:**
For each of these, here's what gets your CIM passed on immediately:

- NRR below 90%
- Declining EBITDA margins
- Founder on 50%+ of client accounts
- One client above 30% of revenue
- No management layer between founder and staff

Two or more of these? You're not ready.

**Tweet 8:**
Self-audit today:

Grade yourself 1-5 on each of these five criteria.

Anything below a 3 needs to be fixed before going to market. And most fixes take 12-18 months.

Start now. Not when you're ready to sell.

Where do you score lowest?
