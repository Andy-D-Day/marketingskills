# Post 9 — 3 Years Preparing to Sell (Here's What I'd Do Differently)

**Audience:** Seller
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

I spent 3 years "preparing" to sell my agency. Here's what I wish I had done in the first 3 months instead.

Year 1 was spent "thinking about it." I told myself I was being strategic. In reality, I was procrastinating. I read blog posts about exits. I had coffee with a couple of brokers. I attended one M&A conference. I did zero actual preparation. That year was a total waste.

Year 2 was fixing financials that should have been clean from day one. My books were a disaster — personal expenses mixed with business expenses, inconsistent revenue recognition, no real tracking of adjusted EBITDA. I spent $40K on a fractional CFO and a new bookkeeper just to get 24 months of clean financials together. If I'd set this up properly from the start, it would have taken 2 months, not 12.

Year 3 was actually going to market — which should have been month 6. Finding the right advisor, building the CIM, running the process, negotiating LOIs. This was the only year that actually moved the needle, and even then I realized I'd over-prepared on things buyers didn't care about (a 40-page culture deck) and under-prepared on things they scrutinized relentlessly (client concentration, founder dependency, contract terms).

If I could do it all over again, I'd compress everything into a 90-day sprint:

**Month 1: Clean the financials.** Hire a fractional CFO or a CPA who specializes in agency accounting. Get your adjusted EBITDA calculated correctly. Separate personal from business. Recast your P&L for the last three years. This is non-negotiable — it's the first thing every buyer asks for.

**Month 2: Document processes and diversify revenue.** Write SOPs for your top 10 workflows. Identify your client concentration risk (no single client over 15% of revenue). If you're over-indexed on one service line, start building a second. Begin transitioning key client relationships to senior team members.

**Month 3: Hire a second-in-command and talk to advisors.** If you don't have someone who can run the business for 90 days without you, promote your best operator or hire one externally. Simultaneously, start conversations with 2-3 M&A advisors to understand your market positioning and timeline.

Most sellers over-prepare on branding and narrative — the "story" of their agency. Buyers don't care about your story. They care about clean financials, diversified revenue, and a business that doesn't depend on you to function.

If you're thinking about selling in 2+ years, start today. Not "start thinking about it." Start doing it.

What's the one thing holding you back from starting your exit prep?
