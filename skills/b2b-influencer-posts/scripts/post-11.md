# Post 11 — The Worst Thing That Happened Was Crossing $3M

**Audience:** Entrepreneur
**Platform Scripts:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X

---

## LinkedIn Script

The worst thing that happened to your agency was crossing $3M. Here's what I mean.

At $1-3M, you're unstoppable. You sell, deliver, and manage every client relationship personally. Margins are healthy because your sweat equity fills every gap and overhead is low.

Then you hit $3M and everything breaks.

You start hiring — not strategically, but reactively. A PM here, a strategist there, an ops person because things slip through cracks. Revenue grows but profit flatlines. You went from 25% margins at $2M to 12% at $3.5M. More revenue, same take-home.

Welcome to no man's land: $3-5M.

Too big to be scrappy. Too small to have real infrastructure. You're paying 15-20 people but still doing 40% of the critical work yourself.

The founders who break through $5M all make three shifts at $3M:

1. Doer to manager. They come off client accounts entirely.
2. Generalist to specialist. "We do everything" becomes one thing done exceptionally well.
3. Reactive to systematized. Onboarding, delivery, QA, reporting — all documented.

The irony: the skills that got you to $3M — hustle, flexibility, doing it yourself — are exactly what traps you there.

The founders who break through are willing to rebuild the business at $3M. Not tweak it. Rebuild it.

If you're stuck between $3-5M, what's the thing that feels hardest to change?
