# No Nasties — Brand Identity

*Created: 2026-02-26*

---

## 1. Brand Essence

**No Nasties exists to make clean, chemical-free living feel effortless and aspirational for health-conscious consumers who refuse to compromise on style.**

---

## 2. Brand Positioning Statement

For health-conscious consumers who want to live clean without looking like they're trying too hard, **No Nasties** is the curated wellness brand that combines research-backed, chemical-free products with a fashion-forward aesthetic — because clean living should look as good as it feels.

---

## 3. Brand Values

### Transparency
- **What it means:** Every product is backed by research. Ingredients, sourcing, and standards are always visible. No fine print, no greenwashing.
- **What it doesn't mean:** Preachy, lecturing, or guilt-tripping. We inform, we don't shame.

### Clean Authority
- **What it means:** We are a trusted resource. We do the homework so our customers don't have to. If it's in our range, it's been vetted.
- **What it doesn't mean:** Clinical or sterile. We're authoritative but warm — a knowledgeable friend, not a lab coat.

### Effortless Style
- **What it means:** Clean living is beautiful, modern, and desirable. Our aesthetic is editorial, not earthy.
- **What it doesn't mean:** Superficial or trend-chasing. Style serves substance. We look good because we are good.

### Conscious Living
- **What it means:** Respect for body, environment, and longevity. Every choice considers long-term wellbeing and environmental impact.
- **What it doesn't mean:** Militant, restrictive, or judgemental. We make the better choice feel like the easy choice.

She reads *Kinfolk* and *Vogue*. She knows what adaptogens are but doesn't make it her whole personality. ### Brand as a Magazine
A cross between *Kinfolk* (aesthetic restraint, natural beauty) and *Vogue* (fashion authority, aspiration) — with the wellness credibility of *Well+Good*.

## 5. Visual Identity Direction

### Color Palette

#### Primary Colors
| Color | Hex (approx.) | Role |
|-------|---------------|------|
| **Pale Sky Blue** | `#B8D4E3` | Trust, clarity, clean water — the brand's signature calm |
| **Soft Blush Pink** | `#F2D4D0` | Warmth, approachability, femininity without fragility |
| **Sage Green** | `#C5D5C0` | Nature, wellness, growth — grounding without being earthy |

#### Secondary Colors
| Color | Hex (approx.) | Role |
|-------|---------------|------|
| **Lavender Mist** | `#D8D0E8` | Luxury, premium feel, modernity |
| **Warm Sand** | `#F0E6D3` | Neutral warmth, beach reference, natural elegance |
| **Soft White** | `#FAFAF7` | Clean space, breathing room, purity |

#### Neutral Palette
| Color | Hex (approx.) | Role |
|-------|---------------|------|
| **Charcoal** | `#2D2D2D` | Primary text — softer than black, more sophisticated |
| **Warm Grey** | `#9B9B93` | Secondary text, subtle elements |
| **Off-White** | `#FAFAF7` | Backgrounds — warm, not sterile |

#### Color Rationale
The palette draws directly from the brand's natural references: ocean water (pale blue), wildflowers and skin (blush pink), meadow fields (sage green), and tropical florals (lavender). These are deliberately **pale and muted** — never saturated or loud. The palette communicates cleanliness and nature without veering into "health food store" territory. The lavender adds a fashion-forward edge that separates No Nasties from the typical green-and-brown wellness aesthetic.

### Typography Direction

- **Headings:** A refined sans-serif with subtle character — think along the lines of *Futura*, *Celine*-style clean type, or a modern geometric sans. Elegant and editorial. Uppercase tracking for short display headlines; title case for longer headlines.
- **Body:** A highly readable sans-serif with generous line height. Clean, airy, modern. Something in the family of *Aktiv Grotesk*, *Söhne*, or *Inter*.
- **Accent (optional):** A delicate serif for pull quotes, callouts, or product descriptions — to add editorial warmth. Think *Cormorant Garamond* or *Freight Text*.
- **Overall mood:** The typography should feel like a fashion magazine layout — plenty of white space, considered hierarchy, never cramped.

### Photography & Imagery Style

**Subject matter:**
- Natural ingredients, botanicals, and textures (close-up, editorial)
- Clean, styled flat lays of products on natural surfaces (marble, linen, light wood)
- Lifestyle moments: morning light, ocean swims, fresh food, calm interiors
- Aspirational but attainable — real people in beautiful but not unrelatable settings

**Mood and lighting:**
- Soft, natural light — golden hour, diffused daylight
- Airy and bright, never moody or dark
- Warm undertones — not cold or clinical

**Color treatment:**
- Muted, desaturated pastels — colors should feel lifted and gentle
- Never overly filtered or artificially warm
- Consistent with the brand palette (pale blues, soft pinks, greens)

**Composition:**
- Clean and considered — editorial layouts with breathing room
- Negative space is a feature, not a gap
- Overhead and 45-degree angles for product shots
- Environmental shots that feel discovered, not posed

**What to avoid:**
- Dark, moody imagery
- Overly saturated or neon colors
- Cluttered, busy compositions
- Stock photography feel — nothing generic
- "Hippy" or "bohemian" visual codes: tie-dye, macrame, dreamcatchers, unpolished textures
- Clinical or pharmaceutical-looking imagery
- Gym or intense fitness imagery

### Texture & Pattern
- **Organic and soft** — linen weaves, water ripples, gentle botanical illustrations
- **Matte over glossy** for printed materials — with selective gloss for premium accents
- **Minimal pattern use** — if patterns appear, they should be subtle and nature-derived (watercolor washes, soft gradients, delicate line botanicals)
- No heavy geometric patterns, no tribal or ethnic prints, no busy repeats

## 6. Brand Voice

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Tone** | Warm confidence. Like a knowledgeable friend who never lectures. |
| **Vocabulary** | Modern, clean, precise. Uses wellness language naturally but never as jargon. Accessible to someone new to clean living. |
| **Rhythm** | Short, clear sentences. Occasional longer sentence for warmth. Avoids breathless lists of buzzwords. |
| **Perspective** | Second person ("you") for customer-facing. First person plural ("we") for brand statements. |

### Voice Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "We researched 40 sunscreens to find 3 worth recommending." | "All sunscreens are toxic! Here's what you should use instead." |
| "Clean ingredients, beautiful results." | "OMG this product is AMAZING and literally changed my life!!!" |
| "Your skin deserves better." | "You need to stop putting chemicals on your face." |
| Use straightforward, confident language | Use fear-based messaging or scare tactics |
| Reference research and evidence naturally | Over-cite studies or sound like a scientific paper |
| Be aspirational and inviting | Be exclusive, gatekeeping, or judgmental |
| Mention sustainability as a natural part of quality | Make sustainability the entire message |

### Language Guidelines

**Words to use:** clean, researched, trusted, curated, effortless, intentional, quality, mindful, radiant, nourishing, refined, considered

**Words to avoid:** toxic (as a fear word), chemical-free (scientifically inaccurate — prefer "free from harmful chemicals"), all-natural (overused, vague), guilt-free, superfood (unless specific), detox (unless clinically accurate), clean eating (loaded term), holistic (overused), organic (unless certified), hippy, crunchy, granola

**Preferred phrasing:**
- "Free from harmful chemicals" over "chemical-free"
- "Research-backed" over "scientifically proven"
- "Made with care" over "handmade" (unless literally handmade)
- "Better choices" over "the right choices"

## 7. Brand Narrative

There's a gap in the market — and it's shaped like a question most people are quietly asking: *What am I actually putting on my skin, in my home, in my body?*

The clean living space is full of answers, but most of them come wrapped in guilt, jargon, or an aesthetic that feels like it belongs in a different decade. On one side, there's the wellness world — well-intentioned but often unpolished, unverified, and speaking in a language that only converts already believe. On the other, there's the beauty and lifestyle industry — glossy, desirable, and powered by ingredients lists that require a chemistry degree to decode.

**No Nasties sits in the space between.** We do the research — properly — so you don't have to. Every product we recommend or sell has been vetted for what's in it and what's not. But we don't think clean living should come with a uniform. No Nasties looks like the brands you already love — the fashion labels, the design studios, the editorial titles that set the standard for modern taste. We just happen to believe that what's inside matters as much as what's outside. Clean, researched, beautiful. No compromises. No nasties.

## 8. Audience Archetype

### The Conscious Curator

**Demographics:** 25-42, predominantly female (but not exclusively), urban or semi-urban, mid-to-high disposable income, educated.

**Lifestyle:** She invests in quality over quantity. Her home is considered — not minimalist for minimalism's sake, but intentionally curated. She cooks with good ingredients, moves her body regularly (pilates, ocean swims, long walks — not punishing gym sessions). She travels to places for their culture and nature, not just their nightlife.

**Values:** Health as a long-term investment. Environmental responsibility as a baseline, not a badge. Aesthetic quality as non-negotiable. Transparency and honesty from the brands she supports.

**Aspirations:** To age well. To live in an environment that feels clean and beautiful. To make better choices without it feeling like a project. To be the friend who always knows the best version of everything.

**Media diet:** *Kinfolk*, *Vogue*, *The New York Times Wirecutter*, *Well+Good*, *Goop* (but slightly skeptical of it). Follows curated Instagram accounts for interiors, food, and fashion. Listens to wellness-adjacent podcasts but skips anything too "woo."

**Brands she loves:** Aesop, Le Labo, Glossier, Reformation, Patagonia, Byredo, The Laundress, Seed, Dr. Barbara Sturm.

**What turns her off:**
- Fear-based marketing ("Your shampoo is killing you!")
- Preachy or judgmental tone
- Cheap-looking design or packaging
- Brands that feel like they're trying too hard to be "natural"
- MLM / direct sales energy
- Unsubstantiated health claims
- Anything that looks or feels "hippy"

## 10. Designer Brief

```
PROJECT: No Nasties — Brandmark & Logo Design

BRAND ESSENCE:
No Nasties makes clean, chemical-free living effortless and
aspirational — researched, trusted, and fashion-forward.

POSITIONING:
The curated wellness brand where research meets editorial
aesthetics. Clean living that looks as good as it feels.

PERSONALITY:
Confident, warm, refined, modern, credible

VISUAL DIRECTION:
- Primary colors: Pale Sky Blue (#B8D4E3), Soft Blush Pink
  (#F2D4D0), Sage Green (#C5D5C0)
- Secondary: Lavender Mist (#D8D0E8), Warm Sand (#F0E6D3)
- Typography feel: Clean sans-serif with editorial elegance.
  Think Celine, Glossier, Kinfolk — modern, spacious, refined.
- Imagery references: Açai bowls on marble, fields of
  wildflowers in soft focus, white sand meeting rainforest
  edge, still pale-blue water
- Overall aesthetic: Glossy fashion magazine meets clean
  wellness authority

THE BRAND IS:
- Fashion-forward and editorially styled
- Warm, confident, and trustworthy
- Research-backed and credible
- Clean, calm, and aspirational
- Modern and refined

THE BRAND IS NOT:
- Hippy, bohemian, or "crunchy"
- Clinical, cold, or pharmaceutical
- Loud, busy, or saturated
- Preachy, fear-based, or judgmental
- Rustic, handmade, or DIY

BRANDMARK CONSIDERATIONS:
- Must work at small sizes (favicon, app icon, product labels)
- Must work in single color (for packaging embossing, foil stamps)
- Should feel refined and premium, not hand-drawn or casual
- The mark should communicate "clean" and "trusted" —
  think purity, clarity, lightness
- Consider: clean wordmark (primary), symbol/icon (secondary),
  combination mark
- A wordmark-first approach is recommended — the name
  "No Nasties" is distinctive and memorable, and the
  typography itself can carry the brand
- If an icon is developed, draw from: water, a leaf,
  a clean circle/droplet, or an abstract "shield" of trust
- Avoid: overly literal icons (no crossed-out skull and
  crossbones), hearts, generic leaf-in-circle marks,
  or anything that reads as "organic food store"

REFERENCE BRANDS (for style direction, not imitation):
- Glossier — clean, modern, pink-toned, approachable luxury
- Aesop — typographic confidence, understated premium feel
- Kinfolk — editorial restraint, natural color palette, space
- Byredo — minimal, fashion-credible, quiet luxury
- Reformation — sustainable but stylish, never preachy

DELIVERABLES:
- Primary logo (horizontal wordmark)
- Stacked/vertical variant
- Brandmark/icon (standalone, for favicon and social)
- Single-color versions (black on white, white on dark)
- Reversed versions for colored backgrounds
- Minimum size specifications
- Clear space rules
- Color palette specification (Pantone, CMYK, RGB, Hex)
```

## 11. Next Steps

1. **Trademark search:** Run a trademark search for "No Nasties" in relevant classes (cosmetics, wellness, lifestyle goods) in target markets before investing in design. Check existing registrations and common law use.

2. **Designer selection:** Brief 2-3 brand identity designers. Look for portfolios with fashion, beauty, or premium wellness work. Share this document as the creative brief.

3. **Mood board refinement:** Create a visual mood board with 15-20 reference images aligned to the photography direction above. Share with your designer alongside this brief.

4. **Brand guidelines document:** After the logo is finalized, commission a full brand guidelines PDF covering logo usage, color specs, typography hierarchy, photography standards, and brand voice rules.
