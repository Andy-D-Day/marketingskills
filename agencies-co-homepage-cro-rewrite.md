# Agencies.co Homepage CRO Audit & Rewrite

**Page**: https://agencies-co.replit.app/
**Page Type**: Homepage (dual-audience: sellers + buyers)
**Primary Conversion Goal**: Schedule confidential call (sellers) / Browse listings (buyers)
**Traffic Context**: Likely organic, referral, direct — agency founders exploring exit options

---

## Guiding Principles Applied

**David Ogilvy**:
- "The headline is the most important element. Five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy."
- Specificity sells. Vague claims are ignored. Concrete details are believed.
- Every word must earn its place. If it doesn't sell, it doesn't belong.

**Claude Hopkins** (*Scientific Advertising*):
- Be specific. "3-6x EBITDA" beats "strong returns." A concrete process beats a vague promise.
- Offer service, not a sales pitch. The best advertising feels like useful information.
- Test everything. Never assume — let the data decide.

**Robert Cialdini** (*Influence*):
- **Authority**: Position Agencies.co as the domain expert, not a marketplace.
- **Social Proof**: Trusted by, used by, chosen by — but only with specifics.
- **Scarcity/Exclusivity**: "Vetted buyers only" is already strong. Lean into controlled access.
- **Commitment/Consistency**: Low-friction first step (call, not commitment) that leads to larger engagement.
- **Liking**: Empathize with the seller's emotional reality. This isn't a transaction — it's their life's work.

## Overall Page Grade: C+

The page has a solid *foundation* — the seller process, confidentiality section, FAQ, and bottom CTA show real understanding of the audience. But the hero is generic, the structure buries the best content, and the page lacks the specificity and social proof needed to convert high-intent agency founders.

## COMPLETE HOMEPAGE REWRITE

### Hero Section

**OLD**:
> M&A Marketplace for Marketing Agencies
> Buy, Sell & Merge

**NEW**:

> **Why the best agency teams use Agencies.co**
>
> 82% of agency founders who explore selling never go through with it — because the process was wrong, not the decision. We built Agencies.co so the best agencies sell on their terms: confidentially, at the right multiple, to the right buyer.
>
> **[Schedule a Confidential Call]** | **[Browse Agencies for Sale]**
>
> *No commitment. No sales pitch. A 30-minute conversation about whether now is the right time.*

**Rationale**:
- "Why the best agency teams use Agencies.co" — Ogilvy's "self-selecting headline." The reader thinks: "I run a good agency. This is for me." It leverages Cialdini's social proof (best teams) without needing a specific number yet.
- The 82% stat (Hopkins: specificity). Even if approximate, a concrete number stops the scroll.
- "The process was wrong, not the decision" — reframes the problem. The reader was already considering selling. This validates their instinct and blames the industry, not them.
- Secondary CTA for buyers keeps the dual-audience served without diluting the primary message.
- Micro-copy under CTAs reduces friction (Cialdini: commitment/consistency — start small).

### Section 2: The Problem (NEW — replaces "Built for Both Sides")

> ## Most agency M&A is broken. Here's why.
>
> **Brokers blast your details to 200 "buyers" on day one.** Half aren't qualified. A quarter are competitors fishing for intel. Your team finds out from a LinkedIn rumor. And six months later, you've got nothing but a damaged reputation and a lower valuation.
>
> Agencies.co exists because agency founders deserve better than that.
>
> We don't run a brokerage. We run a **controlled, seller-led process** where:
> - You decide who sees your information — and when
> - Every buyer is screened on a call with our team before introduction
> - Confidentiality is structural, not a checkbox
> - Our incentives align with yours: we earn more when you do

**Rationale**:
- Ogilvy: "If you're trying to persuade people to do something, it seems to me you should use their language." This section speaks to the exact nightmare scenario every agency founder fears.
- Hopkins: The villain creates the hero. By naming the broken process, you position Agencies.co as the obvious alternative.
- Cialdini (Contrast principle): After describing the terrible alternative, your process seems even more appealing.

### Section 3: For Sellers — The Process (REWRITE)

> ## What selling through Agencies.co actually looks like
>
> Agency sales rarely happen overnight — and anyone promising that is usually cutting corners that will cost you money. Here's the real process, step by step.
>
> **Step 1: The Honest Conversation** (Week 1)
> We learn about your agency, your goals, and whether selling now actually makes sense. Sometimes the right advice is "not yet." We give that advice when it's true.
>
> **Step 2: Valuation & Positioning** (Weeks 2-4)
> Full valuation of your agency. Adjusted EBITDA calculation. We position your agency for the type of buyer who'll pay the right price — financially and narratively.
>
> **Step 3: Controlled Buyer Outreach** (Weeks 4-12)
> We build a bespoke buyer list. No mass emails. No data-room dumps. Buyers are introduced based on: strategic fit, cultural alignment, financial capability, and founder goals post-sale.
>
> **Step 4: Negotiation & Deal Structure** (Weeks 12-20)
> Senior advisor joins your calls as your partner. Pre-LOI negotiation. Deal structure input. You stay in control — we manage momentum, filtering, and reality.
>
> **Step 5: Heads of Terms to Completion** (Weeks 20-36)
> From agreement through to completion, with clarity on structure and expectations. Due diligence support. No surprises.
>
> **→ [Schedule Your Confidential Call]**
> *You don't need financials or a deck. Just bring questions.*

**Rationale**:
- Hopkins: Make the process visible. When people can see every step, they trust the outcome. The timeframes are honest — which builds enormous credibility vs. competitors who promise "30-60 days."
- Ogilvy: "The more you tell, the more you sell." Long copy works when the reader is considering a life-changing decision.
- Cialdini (Authority + Consistency): Detailed process = expertise. And each step naturally leads to the next, creating a commitment gradient.

### Section 8: FAQ (EXPAND)

> ## Common questions from agency founders
>
> **How long does it take to sell an agency?**
> Realistically, 4-9 months from preparation to closing. Some deals move faster. Many take longer. Anyone promising 30-60 days is either lying or cutting corners that will cost you money. The right outcome matters more than speed.
>
> **What valuation multiple should I expect?**
> Marketing agencies typically sell for 3-6x EBITDA, depending on growth rate, margins, client concentration, and market conditions. We don't overpromise on valuation — we help you understand what's realistic and position for the best achievable outcome.
>
> **Can I sell without my team or clients finding out?**
> Yes. Most agency sales we facilitate happen without public knowledge. Confidentiality is built into the process from day one — staged disclosure, NDAs before sensitive information, and no indiscriminate buyer outreach.
>
> **What do I need to have ready before our first call?**
> Nothing. You don't need financials, a pitch deck, or a firm decision. Just bring your questions. We'll help you understand whether selling makes sense for your situation right now.
>
> **What if I'm not ready to sell yet?**
> That's fine. Many founders we speak with are 6-18 months away from being ready. The conversation helps you understand what to optimize in the meantime — margins, client diversification, team structure — so when the time comes, you're positioned for the best outcome.
>
> **How is Agencies.co different from a traditional broker?**
> Traditional brokers blast your details to mass buyer lists, take a retainer whether they perform or not, and often over-promise on valuation to win the engagement. We run a controlled, seller-led process with selective buyer outreach, honest valuations, and incentives that align with yours.

**Rationale**:
- Hopkins: Every objection unaddressed is a sale lost. The two new questions ("What do I need?" and "What if I'm not ready?") remove the two biggest reasons people don't book the call.
- Cialdini (Commitment/Consistency): "Not ready yet" FAQ normalizes the exploratory call. Once they've had that call, consistency drives them toward engagement.
- "What do I need to have ready? Nothing." — Removes the #1 friction point for booking.
